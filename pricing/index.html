What it costs, worked out

Repository pricing is simple, but nobody should have to do arithmetic to find out what a product costs:

8 repositories          → below the $10 minimum   →  $10 / month
30 repositories         → 30 × $2                 →  $60 / month
30 repos + Champions    → $60 + $29 add-on        →  $89 / month
80 repositories         → 80 × $3                 →  $240 / month
200 repositories        → 200 × $4                →  $800 / month

Same price with 3 developers or 300.

The $10 minimum is why very small teams pay $10 rather than $0.99 × their repository count. It's the floor, not a surprise. Optional add-ons (Champions $29, V.AI $39) stack on top.

COMMERCIAL QUESTIONS

Before you ask

Why price by repository instead of by developer?

Because charging per developer punishes you for growing the team, and your risk doesn't scale with headcount — it scales with the code you own. Ten engineers on twelve repositories carry roughly the same application risk as three engineers on the same twelve.

Are any features held back on cheaper plans?

No — every plan is the full core: risk score, pull request analysis, timeline and normalization are all there. The subscription price changes with how many repositories you connect. The only thing that adds to it is an optional module (Champions or V.AI), and only if you choose one.

What happens if my repository count changes?

The tier follows the repositories you have connected: add repositories and you move up a tier, disconnect them and you move back down. No annual contract, no penalty — you pay for what you're actually analyzing.

When does it make sense to move to Enterprise?

When you have hundreds or thousands of repositories and per-repository list pricing stops being the right conversation — then pricing moves to active, relevant repositories, with SSO, dedicated support and onboarding. See how enterprise works →
